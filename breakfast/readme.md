# Arroz con Leche (Mexican Cinnamon Rice Pudding)

## Description

A warm, creamy rice pudding flavored with cinnamon and citrus peel, commonly eaten for breakfast in Mexico alongside pan dulce or café de olla. It descends from Persian and Arab milk-rice dishes (like *shir berenj*), which the Moors brought to Spain starting in the 8th century; Spanish colonizers carried it to the Americas in the 16th century, where it picked up Mexican touches like *canela* and sweetened condensed milk.

### Ingredients:
- [ ] 2 cup long-grain white rice
- [ ] 4 cups water
- [ ] 2 cinnamon stick (Mexican/Ceylon *canela* if possible)
- [ ] 2 strip lime or orange peel
- [ ] Pinch of salt
- [ ] 8 cups whole milk
- [ ] 2 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk (or ½ cup sugar)
- [ ] 2 tsp vanilla extract
- [ ] Ground cinnamon, for topping
- [ ] 1/2 cup raisins (optional)

### Steps for preparation:

1. Place the rice in a strainer and rinse under cold water until the water runs mostly clear. If you have time, soak the rice in water for 20 minutes, then drain.
2. In a medium pot, combine the rice, 2 cups water, cinnamon stick, citrus peel, and salt. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
3. Lower the heat to a simmer and cook uncovered for about 15 minutes, until most of the water has been absorbed.
4. Pour in the milk and stir. Keep the heat at medium-low and simmer for 20–25 minutes, stirring every few minutes so the rice doesn't stick to the bottom, until the rice is soft.
5. Stir in the sweetened condensed milk (or sugar) and the raisins, if using. Cook for 5 more minutes, stirring, until the mixture is thick and creamy.
6. Turn off the heat. Remove and discard the cinnamon stick and citrus peel, then stir in the vanilla.
7. Spoon into bowls and sprinkle with ground cinnamon. Serve warm.

### Notes:

- The pudding thickens as it cools. To reheat, add a splash of milk and stir over low heat.
- For a dairy-free version, use oat or coconut milk and skip the condensed milk in favor of sugar.
- It's also served chilled as a dessert throughout Latin America and Spain.
